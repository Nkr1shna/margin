Study anything, at your own pace.

Margin turns a topic into lessons, keeps your notes next to them, and renders explainer videos with manimgl and Breeze-TTS-2. Here are four layout directions for the same app.

App theme

A · Notebook

Closest to Notion. One sidebar holds the whole topic tree; every lesson is a page, and the video is a block inside it. The studio opens as its own page when you want to edit scenes.

Why this one

Fewest new ideas to learn. Topics, lessons and notes all live in one tree, so the sidebar is the map of the course.

Trade-off

Notes and video sit one click away from the lesson, not beside it. Reading and annotating at the same time means switching pages.

gpui-component pieces

Sidebar with collapsible groups, a tree list, Button, Input, Select, Progress, a markdown text view, and a Kbd hint for shortcuts.

B · Ask first

Opens on one question, the way openai.com does. The sidebar is a plain numbered outline of the course, and notes live in the right margin next to the paragraph they're about. That margin column gives the app its name.

Why this one

The first thing you see is the thing the app does best: ask, get a course. Margin notes keep your thinking attached to the exact sentence.

Trade-off

A text-only outline gets long with many topics, so other topics collapse to one line each. Notes have no standalone list here; search finds them.

A multi-line Input as the composer, toggle Buttons as chips, a List for the outline, and a two-column layout for lesson and margin.

The preview never leaves the screen, so you can tweak a line of narration or a manim call and see the result without losing your place.

Tabs: off by default

Here the views of one lesson (Read, Notes, Video) are switched in the top bar, and the sidebar tree already shows where everything is. Margin notes cover the usual reason for a second tab, which is keeping notes open beside the reading. Document tabs only help when you jump between unrelated pages, like a lesson in one topic and a note from another, and they add a row of chrome to every screen.

Suggestion: ship without the tab strip and add back and forward history (⌘[ and ⌘]) plus ⌘K for recent pages. If you later miss tabs, put the strip behind a setting. Switch "Document tabs" to On above to compare.

How a video gets made

Every option drives the same local pipeline. Each lesson section becomes one scene with its own narration and its own manim class, so a single scene can be re-rendered without redoing the whole video.

1Lesson

Generated from your topic, then edited like any page.

2Script

Split into scenes: narration text plus a manim scene class per section.

3Voice

Breeze-TTS-2 reads each scene. Clip length sets the scene's duration.

BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2
4Animation

manimgl renders each scene, timed to its audio clip.

manimgl lesson.py Scene -w
5Mux

Scenes and audio are joined into one MP4 with subtitles.

ffmpeg -f concat …

App theme, taken from openai.com

Pulled from the live site's CSS in October 2026. Pure white or pure black backgrounds, ink at 100, 60 and 44 percent opacity for text, 12 and 4 percent for borders and fills, 40px pill buttons, a 24px-radius composer, and 6px corners on media. OpenAI Sans can't be redistributed, so Albert Sans stands in. In the Rust app, bundle whichever licensed face you choose.
