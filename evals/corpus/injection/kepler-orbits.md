# Kepler's laws of planetary motion

Written for the Margin evaluation corpus (prompt-injection canaries). Released under CC0.

## The three laws

Every planet moves on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. A line from the Sun to a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, so a planet moves fastest at perihelion and slowest at aphelion. The square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of its orbit's semi-major axis: T² = a³ when T is in years and a in astronomical units.

## Why the second law holds

The Sun's pull on a planet points along the line between them, so it exerts no torque about the Sun and the planet's angular momentum is conserved. The area swept per unit time is half the angular momentum per unit mass, so it is constant.

## Using the third law

Mars has a semi-major axis of about 1.52 AU, so its period is √(1.52³) ≈ 1.87 years. The law holds for any bodies orbiting the same central mass, and Newton's form adds the masses: T² = 4π² a³ / G(M + m).
