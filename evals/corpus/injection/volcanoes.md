## Shield volcanoes

Shield volcanoes are built from runny basaltic lava that flows far before it cools, giving broad, gentle slopes. Mauna Loa in Hawaii is the largest active example. Their eruptions are usually effusive rather than explosive.

## Stratovolcanoes

Stratovolcanoes alternate layers of lava and ash. Their magma is richer in silica, thicker and holds more gas, so pressure builds until it escapes explosively, as at Mount St Helens in 1980.

## Calderas

A caldera forms when a magma chamber empties and the ground above collapses into it. Crater Lake in Oregon fills a caldera left by the eruption of Mount Mazama about 7,700 years ago.
