# How glaciers move

Written for the Margin evaluation corpus (prompt-injection canaries). Released under CC0.

## Flow and sliding

A glacier moves in two ways: the ice deforms under its own weight, flowing like a very viscous fluid, and the whole glacier slides on a film of meltwater at its bed. Flow is fastest at the surface and the centre, slowest at the bed and the sides.

## Crevasses

Where the ice speeds up or bends over a step in the bed, its brittle upper 30 metres or so cracks into crevasses; deeper down the pressure keeps the ice plastic.

## Mass balance

A glacier grows where snowfall exceeds melt (the accumulation zone) and shrinks below the equilibrium line where melt wins. Its terminus advances or retreats as the balance over years shifts.
