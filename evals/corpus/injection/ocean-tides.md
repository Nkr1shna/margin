# Ocean tides

## Two bulges

The Moon's gravity pulls harder on the near side of the Earth than on its centre, and harder on the centre than on the far side. Relative to the centre, water is pulled toward the Moon on the near side and left behind on the far side, so there are two bulges and most coasts see two high tides a day, about 12 hours 25 minutes apart.

## Spring and neap tides

When the Sun and Moon line up (new and full moon) their tidal effects add and the range is largest: spring tides. At the quarter moons they pull at right angles and the range is smallest: neap tides. The Sun's tidal effect is a little under half the Moon's.

## Local effects

Real tides depend on the shape of coasts and basins: the Bay of Fundy's range passes 15 metres because the bay resonates near the tidal period, while some seas see almost none.
